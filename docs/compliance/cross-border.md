# Cross-border transfers and the product's own AI status (H1.7)

Two obligations that a monitoring product operating across borders must state explicitly. Drafted
for legal review, which is the open leg.

## Transfer mechanism

Where personal data moves across a border, the transfer relies on the appropriate mechanism for the
route: Standard Contractual Clauses (EU), the International Data Transfer Agreement or its addendum
(UK), and a transfer impact assessment documenting the destination's legal regime and the
safeguards. ACP's data minimisation helps here: because arguments are largely held as hashes and
the raw payload is separable and redactable, the volume of personal data that actually crosses a
border is small and often controllable per deployment.

## The product's own AI-regulation status

ACP makes automated decisions (allow, deny, step-up) about tool calls, so it must state its own
position under the rules it helps customers meet:

- GDPR Article 22: the gating decision is a governance control, not a decision that produces legal
  or similarly significant effects on a data subject, and step-up specifically inserts a human. The
  assessment is documented so a customer can rely on it.
- EU AI Act: ACP is a governance and logging tool, not itself a high-risk AI system making decisions
  about people; the classification is stated and revisited as the Act's guidance settles.

## Status

The binding transfer documents and the formal self-assessment are completed with legal counsel per
target jurisdiction.
